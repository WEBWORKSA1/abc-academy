A good reading tutor can change a child's school experience. A poor fit wastes money and confidence. Here's what effective early-reading tutoring looks like, what it typically costs, and twelve questions that help you choose well.

Does your child need a tutor?

Tutoring is worth considering when:

  • Your child's teacher says they're behind in reading benchmarks.
  • Your child has practiced regularly at home but progress has stalled.
  • Reading has become a source of stress or avoidance.
  • Your child has a diagnosed learning difference and needs specialized instruction.
  • You want to keep skills strong over the summer.

What good early-reading tutoring looks like

  • Assessment first: the tutor checks letter sounds, phonemic awareness, decoding and sight words before planning.
  • Systematic phonics: skills are taught in a clear sequence, not randomly.
  • Explicit teaching: the tutor models, practices with the child, then lets the child try alone.
  • Lots of reading: every session includes reading connected text, not just games.
  • Progress tracking: you get regular updates with specific skills and measures.
  • Warmth: your child looks forward to sessions.

What does it cost?

Prices vary a lot by country, city and qualifications. As a rough guide in North America, general tutoring from students or newer tutors often sits at the lower end, certified teachers charge more, and specialists trained in structured-literacy approaches typically charge the most. Small-group sessions and online options are usually less expensive per hour. Always ask for the total cost of a recommended program, not just the hourly rate.

12 questions to ask a tutor

  1. What training do you have in teaching early reading?
  2. How will you assess my child before you start?
  3. What approach or program do you use, and why?
  4. What will a typical session look like?
  5. How often, and for how long, do you recommend sessions?
  6. How will you measure and share progress?
  7. How do you keep young children engaged?
  8. What should we practice at home between sessions?
  9. Do you coordinate with my child's teacher?
  10. Have you worked with children with similar needs?
  11. What happens if we're not seeing progress after a few weeks?
  12. What are the total costs, cancellation policy and contract terms?

Red flags

  • Promises of guaranteed results in a set time
  • No initial assessment
  • Sessions that are mostly worksheets or homework help, with little reading instruction
  • Pressure to sign long, prepaid contracts immediately
  • Vague answers about progress
