The first time a child looks at c-a-t, says the sounds and realizes they just read "cat" is a big moment. CVC words — consonant, vowel, consonant — are where real decoding begins. Here's how to teach blending so it clicks, with word lists to practice.

What are CVC words?

CVC words have three sounds: a consonant, a short vowel and a consonant. Examples: cat, bed, pig, hot, sun. Every letter makes its most common sound, so children can read them using only what they know about single letter sounds.

Before starting, your child should know the sounds of several consonants and at least one or two short vowels. Short a is a common first vowel.

Three ways to teach blending

Sound-by-sound blending

Point under each letter, say its sound, then sweep your finger under the whole word and say it smoothly: "/s/ /a/ /t/… sat!" This is the classic method and works for most children.

Continuous blending

Stretch the sounds together without stopping: "sssaaat". This works best when the word starts with a stretchy sound (s, m, f, l, n, r). It helps children who say the sounds but can't push them together.

Onset + rime (word families)

Teach the ending chunk first: "This says at." Then add a first sound: "/k/… at… cat!" Changing only the first letter (cat, hat, mat, sat) builds speed and confidence. Our word builder is designed around this method.

A 10-minute blending lesson

  1. Warm up (2 min): Quick review of letter sounds with flashcards or the alphabet explorer.
  2. Model (2 min): Blend three words yourself, slowly.
  3. Together (3 min): Blend five words together, then let your child lead.
  4. Apply (3 min): Read a short sentence or a decodable story that uses the same vowel.

When blending won't click

  • Says sounds but not the word: try continuous blending and start with stretchy first sounds.
  • Adds "uh" to sounds ("cuh-a-tuh"): model clipped sounds; it's much easier to blend /k/ /a/ /t/.
  • Guesses from the first letter: cover the word and reveal one letter at a time.
  • Gets tired fast: do two or three words, celebrate, and stop. Short and successful beats long and frustrating.

After CVC words

When CVC words feel easy, move on to words with consonant blends (frog, stop), digraphs (ship, chat) and then magic e (cap → cape). All three are in the phonics lab.
