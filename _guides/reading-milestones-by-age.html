Learning to read is a long journey that starts years before a child reads a word. Knowing the typical milestones helps you choose the right activities — and recognize when it might be worth asking for extra support. Ranges are wide; children often move ahead in one area while taking longer in another.

Ages 2–3: Loving books and language

  • Enjoys being read to and has favorite books
  • Points to and names pictures; turns pages (sometimes several at once)
  • Fills in familiar words in repetitive books
  • Sings songs and nursery rhymes, including the ABC song
  • May recognize a few letters, especially from their name, and familiar logos

Try: read aloud daily, talk about pictures, sing rhymes, play with letters and their sounds.

Ages 3–4: Noticing print and sounds

  • Knows that print carries meaning and "pretend reads" familiar books
  • Recognizes their own name and some letters
  • Starts hearing rhymes and playing with silly words
  • Scribbles and makes letter-like shapes, maybe some real letters
  • Retells parts of a familiar story

Try: rhyme games, clapping syllables, name tracing, letter games.

Ages 4–5: Letters and sounds come together

  • Names most capital letters and many lowercase letters
  • Knows sounds for many letters
  • Hears the first sound in words; can say whether words rhyme
  • Writes their name and some letters; uses invented spellings (KT for cat)
  • Recognizes a handful of common words

Try: letter-sound practice, sound games, simple CVC words.

Ages 5–6 (Kindergarten): Starting to decode

  • Knows all letter names and most letter sounds
  • Blends sounds to read simple CVC words (cat, sun, pig)
  • Reads a growing set of high-frequency words
  • Reads simple decodable books with support
  • Writes simple words and short sentences using letter sounds

Try: the phonics lab, sight words, decodable stories.

Ages 6–7 (Grade 1): Building fluency

  • Reads words with blends, digraphs and long-vowel patterns
  • Reads simple books independently, with growing accuracy
  • Self-corrects when something doesn't make sense
  • Understands and retells what they read
  • Writes several connected sentences

Ages 7–8 (Grade 2): Reading to learn begins

  • Reads multi-syllable words and more complex spelling patterns
  • Reads aloud smoothly with expression
  • Reads chapter books with short chapters
  • Answers questions about why and how things happen in a story
  • Uses reading to find information

When to ask for extra help

Consider talking with your child's teacher or pediatrician if you notice:

  • By the end of kindergarten, your child still knows few letter sounds
  • Difficulty with rhyming or hearing sounds in words that doesn't improve with practice
  • In first grade, frequent guessing from the first letter or pictures instead of sounding out
  • Reading that is very slow and effortful compared with classmates, or strong avoidance of reading
  • A family history of reading difficulties alongside any of the above
