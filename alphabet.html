How to use the alphabet explorer

Children learn letters fastest when they connect three things at once: the letter's shape, its name and the sound it makes. This page does all three in one tap.

  1. Tap and listen. Your child hears "B. B says buh. B is for ball." Ask them to repeat it.
  2. Find the pictures. Tap each picture word and say the first sound together, stretching it out: "bbbb-ball."
  3. Trace big. Start at the top line, follow the dotted letter, and say the sound while tracing.
  4. Do three a day. Three letters a day, revisited often, beats all 26 in one sitting.

Prefer paper? Print a tracing sheet for any letter. Ready for words? Move on to the phonics lab.

Which letters should come first?

Many teachers start with letters that are common and easy to say, such as s, a, t, p, i, n — together they make dozens of short words like sat, pin, tap and nap. Letters in your child's name are another great starting point because they already care about them.

Teach easily-confused pairs like b and d on different days. When both are familiar, compare them side by side.
