What are sight words?

Sight words are the short, very common words that show up again and again in children's books — the, and, said, was, you. Learning to read them instantly frees a young reader to focus on the harder words in a sentence.

Many sight words can be sounded out (and, big, run); others have tricky parts (said, was, one). Point out the regular parts and practice only the tricky bit.

The Dolch lists were compiled by Edward William Dolch from children's books of his time and remain among the most widely used lists in kindergarten through third grade.

A 5-minute daily routine

  1. Flash 5 known words for a quick, confident warm-up.
  2. Add 2–3 new words. Say each, spell it aloud, say it again.
  3. Play one round of the listening quiz.
  4. Find today's words in a real book.
